	Oil painting is a traditional studio medium celebrated for its rich color depth, luminous glazes, and slow drying time. It makes blending a dream but the drying time can also be tedious. Unlike water based mediums, oil paint consists of pigment suspended in a drying oil, typically linseed oil. This slow drying property allows artists to blend smooth gradients and make adjustments over extended working sessions. It works best when you have to start over in an area by scooping with a palette knives. 

## Materials and Setup

Starting with oil paint requires a few fundamental studio tools:

* **Artists' Grade Oil Paints:** Primary colors, titanium white, and earth tones like burnt umber.
*  **Solvent & Mediums:** Odorless mineral spirits for cleanup and thinning, plus linseed oil to increase gloss and fluidity.
* **Support Surface:** Stretched cotton or linen prepared with acrylic gesso (see [[canvas-types-and-surfaces|Canvas Types and Surfaces]]).
* **Palette:** A sturdy wooden, glass, or tear-off paper palette for color mixing.

### The Fat-Over-Lean Rule

The primary rule of oil painting is applying "fat over lean." Initial underpaintings should use paint thinned with solvent (lean). Subsequent layers must contain more oil medium (fat) to ensure the top layers stay flexible and do not crack as lower layers dry.

> "Oil painting requires patience; allowing each layer to cure properly ensures the artwork will endure for centuries."
