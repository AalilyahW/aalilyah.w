# Varnishing and Preservation

Preserving traditional studio artwork requires protecting the finished paint layer from environmental damage, dust, moisture, and ultraviolet light exposure. Varnishing serves as the final defensive barrier for both oil and acrylic paintings.

## Functions of Picture Varnish

Applying a clear protective coat accomplishes two main goals:

1. **Surface Protection:** Acts as a sacrificial layer that absorbs dirt, scratches, and atmospheric pollutants instead of the paint itself.
2. **Visual Unity:** Equalizes uneven sheen across the painting surface, bringing out deep darks and vibrant highlights in a uniform gloss, satin, or matte finish.

### Removable vs. Permanent Varnishes

Fine art varnishes should always be removable using mild solvents, allowing future conservators to clean and restore the painting without damaging the original paint layers.

* **Gamvar Varnish:** Applied once oil paint is hard to the touch.
* **Acrylic Polymer Varnish:** Applied over a dried isolation coat on acrylic works.

> "Varnishing is the final act of stewardship, safeguarding the vibrancy of your artwork for future generations."
